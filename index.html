Geoloc Challenge

A privacy awareness challenge four of us built at INSA Lyon in 2024-2025: a map of people's GPS traces and a quiz on what those traces give away. This page rebuilds the idea with six people who don't exist.

    The challenge

    What you're looking at

    Every dot on the map is a GPS fix: a time and a position, nothing else. That's all an app with location access needs to collect. The original challenge used 1,116,608 real fixes from 10 people, taken from the Priva'Mov campaign (69 volunteers around Lyon, 34.5 million fixes between October 2014 and January 2016). Here, six made-up people spend a made-up week in Lyon, with the flaws of a real log: noise, phones that stop logging for an hour, no signal in the metro and the odd fix a kilometre off.

    To turn fixes into places, the page looks for stays: moments where someone remains within D metres for at least T minutes. Stays at the same spot are merged, then each place gets a label from the list of places the script knows (the original asked OpenStreetMap for an address and a place type). Homes only say "home (approximate)", on purpose.

    With places and times, the rest is reading: where someone sleeps, where they work, who they live with, when they're away. No answer to the quiz is written in the data. It all comes from the traces, the way an app, a data broker or anyone holding a leaked file could get it.

    We tested the original in the fifth-year privacy course at INSA Lyon and showed it to someone from the CNIL, France's data protection authority. They liked the challenge but warned that the participants could be re-identified from their traces. Our report suggested generated data as the fix. This page is that fix.
